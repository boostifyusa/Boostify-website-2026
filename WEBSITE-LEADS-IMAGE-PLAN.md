# Website Leads blog card - image plan

One image. Card art for the homepage blog showcase entry "Why Your Website Isn't
Getting Leads" (`/blog/website-leads`). Card renders at `aspect-[3/4]` under a
bottom-up dark gradient with the white title over the lower third, so the lower
third of the art must stay quiet.

### Image 1 - Leaking funnel (blogcard-website-leads)
**Role:** blogcard-website-leads
**Source:** generate
**Filename:** website-leads.webp
**Alt:** flat illustration of a funnel leaking visitors before any of them convert
**Aspect:** 3:4

```
A flat vector editorial illustration, printed like a two-color risograph poster
with visible paper grain and slightly misregistered ink layers.

The subject: a single large funnel, drawn as a solid flat shape, wide at the top
and narrow at the bottom, sitting in the upper two thirds of a tall portrait
composition. A dense stream of small arrow marks pours down into the wide mouth
of the funnel from the top edge of the frame. The funnel's neck is split open and
broken, so almost all of those arrows escape sideways through the break and
scatter outward as loose falling dots and short dashes that thin out and fade
before they reach the bottom of the frame. Exactly two arrows make it all the way
through the neck and continue downward. Loose hand-drawn scribble marks and
scratchy crosshatching overprint the funnel body, as if sketched with a blunt
crayon over the flat ink.

Composition: tall portrait, the funnel centered in the upper two thirds, and the
bottom third left as calm empty off-white paper with only a few sparse faded dots
so that white text can sit over it.

Style: flat solid fills with hard edges, no gradients inside the shapes, heavy
grain and halftone speckle across the whole image, screenprint texture, confident
and graphic, in the tradition of editorial magazine illustration.

Palette: a loud burnt orange (hex #E8590C) as the dominant ink for the funnel and
the arrows, near-black ink (hex #111111) for the scribble overprint and the
darkest marks, medium gray (hex #666666) for the fading scattered dots, on a true
white paper background (hex #FFFFFF). Only these four colors.

Intended use: the card illustration for a blog article by a web design agency
about websites that get traffic but produce no leads.

No text, no letters, no numbers, no words anywhere in the image. No beige, no
cream, no tan, no purple or blue or lavender AI palette. No gradient text, no
fake UI, no browser windows, no laptops, no phones, no people, no hands, no
glassmorphism, no drop shadows, no 3D render, no stock photography look, no
watermark.
```

### Image 2 - Leaking funnel, landscape (posthero-website-leads)
**Role:** posthero-website-leads

**Filename:** website-leads-hero.webp
**Alt:** flat illustration of a funnel spilling visitors out of a broken neck

```
A flat vector editorial illustration, printed like a two-color risograph poster
with visible paper grain and slightly misregistered ink layers.

The subject: a single large funnel, drawn as a solid flat shape, wide at the top

stream of small arrow marks pours down into the wide mouth of the funnel from the

Composition: wide landscape, the funnel centered and occupying the middle third
horizontally, with the escaping spray filling the left and right thirds so the
frame reads as full edge to edge. Generous calm paper around the funnel mouth.

Style: flat solid fills with hard edges, no gradients inside the shapes, heavy
grain and halftone speckle across the whole image, screenprint texture, confident
and graphic, in the tradition of editorial magazine illustration.

Palette: a loud burnt orange (hex #E8590C) as the dominant ink for the funnel and
the arrows, near-black ink (hex #111111) for the scribble overprint and the
darkest marks, medium gray (hex #666666) for the fading scattered dots, on a true
white paper background (hex #FFFFFF). Only these four colors.

Intended use: the hero banner for a blog article by a web design agency about
websites that get traffic but produce no leads.

No text, no letters, no numbers, no words anywhere in the image. No beige, no
cream, no tan, no purple or blue or lavender AI palette. No gradient text, no
```
