# OSINT Dossier — "Moving Targets vs. Radar Attrition" Debate (2026 Iran War)

**Compiled:** 22 July 2026 · **Method:** open-source sweep (news, official CENTCOM releases, satellite-imagery reporting, Wikipedia conflict pages, ISW/Critical Threats, cloned GitHub OSINT dataset)
**Context:** Reply debate under an @osintdefender post. @can_oe.21 demands the *name and geolocation* of a moving target the US struck "last night" (night of 20–21 July 2026), and asserts Iran destroyed "yet another AN/FPS-117 at the Al-Jaber Air Base in Kuwait," claiming US IAMD coverage is "dwindling day by day."

---

## 1. TL;DR — Who's right?

**Both sides are half right, and the specific claim thrown at @v.stradaa is weaker than it sounds.**

1. **"US destroyed moving targets" is well documented across the war** — named warships sunk at sea, a tanker disabled while underway, 60+ IRGC small boats struck in/near the Strait of Hormuz, and dozens of officially released videos of mobile launcher (TEL) kills. This is not in serious dispute.
2. **For the specific night of 20–21 July**, CENTCOM confirmed it struck "maritime capabilities" and "missile and drone launch sites" but released no per-target BDA. No individually named, geolocated moving-target kill from that night was public within hours — which is normal: official BDA and OSINT geolocation lag strikes by days. Demanding a name + geolocation 8 minutes into the news cycle is a rhetorical trap, not a real evidentiary standard.
3. **The "yet another AN/FPS-117 at Al-Jaber" claim is itself unverified** — and internally suspect. The IRGC claimed to destroy an AN/FPS-117 at Ahmad al-Jaber Air Base on **13 July** and then again on **21 July**. A base operates one such radar; two "destructions" of the same system in 8 days means at least one claim is false (or an unverified replacement was emplaced). No satellite imagery verifying Al-Jaber radar damage has surfaced; Kuwait confirmed only that it engaged "hostile aerial targets," and neither US nor Kuwaiti authorities have confirmed the loss. The user citing burden of proof is simultaneously repeating an unproven IRGC claim.
4. **The broader "US IAMD coverage is dwindling" point has genuine verified support** — satellite imagery has confirmed damage to AN/TPY-2 radars in Jordan and Saudi Arabia and the AN/FPS-132 in Qatar (see §5). But "verified for some radars" ≠ "every IRGC kill claim is true."

### The night in question (20–21 July)

- CENTCOM (9:00 pm ET, 20 Jul): struck "Iranian military command centers, **maritime capabilities**, missile and drone launch sites, and air defense systems to degrade Iran's ability to continue attacking commercial vessels" — no per-target breakdown released.
- ISW/Critical Threats noted US strikes reaching ~200 km inland and interdiction of supply routes to Bandar Abbas (bridges/rail).
- **Honest bottom line:** as of the morning after, there was no publicly named + geolocated *moving* target from that specific night. That neither proves nor disproves the claim — CENTCOM has repeatedly confirmed such kills days later, and imagery providers (Planet, Vantor/Maxar) are under expanded restriction/delay policies for this theater (2-week delay over Iran, Gulf states, and US bases), which structurally slows independent verification of *everything* in this war — including the Al-Jaber claim the other debater relies on.

**Debate-ready reply:** *"Name + geolocation within hours is a standard no claim in this war can meet — including yours. Verified precedent for US kills on moving targets is abundant: IRIS Dena sunk underway near Sri Lanka by a Mk 48; M/T Belma disabled underway off Kharg Island on 15 July; 60+ IRGC small boats struck in the Strait per CENTCOM; dozens of official TEL-kill videos. Meanwhile your 'yet another FPS-117 at Al-Jaber' is an IRGC claim, unverified by any imagery, and it's the second claimed kill of the same radar at the same base in 8 days — at least one of those claims is necessarily false."*

## 4. The Al-Jaber AN/FPS-117 claim — dissection

- **13 Jul 2026:** IRGC claims "complete destruction" of a Patriot battery at Ali Al Salem AB and an **AN/FPS-117** at Ahmad al-Jaber AB. CNN could not verify; Kuwait confirmed only engaging "hostile aerial targets." As of 16 Jul, no US/Kuwaiti damage assessment released.
- **21 Jul 2026:** IRGC (Op. Nasr-2 / Army Op. Sa'eqeh statements, via PressTV/IRNA) claims strikes on Al-Jaber destroying "an early-warning radar, a missile-defense radar, **an FPS-117 radar**, a Patriot system, and a SATCOM system."
- **Contradiction:** the AN/FPS-117 (~470 km range, coverage into Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province) is a fixed long-range surveillance radar; a base fields one. Destroying it "again" 8 days later requires either (a) the 13 Jul claim was false, (b) the 21 Jul claim is false/padded, or (c) an unreported replacement was installed within days — possible but asserted by no one and verified by nothing.
- **No satellite verification exists for Al-Jaber radar damage** in any indexed reporting, in contrast to other sites (§5). Imagery-delay policies cut both ways but currently leave this claim resting solely on IRGC statements.
- Iranian claim tallies compiled by third parties (Türkiye Today's "11 US military sites damaged or destroyed since Feb 28") list **one** AN/FPS-117 among claimed radar kills — consistent with the double-count reading.

## 5. US IAMD attrition — verified vs. claimed (whole war)

### Independently verified (satellite imagery / mainstream investigation)

| System | Location | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **AN/TPY-2** X-band radar | Muwaffaq al-Salti AB, **Jordan** | CNN satellite investigation (5 Mar); Defence Security Asia satellite analysis |
| **AN/TPY-2** radar site | Prince Sultan AB, **Saudi Arabia** | Planet Labs imagery (1 Mar) — smoke from the radar building |
| **AN/FPS-132** early-warning radar (~$1.1B) | Umm Dahal, **Qatar** | Planet Labs imagery; the "$1 billion radar" in Ynet's satellite-image report |
| Radar/base damage | **Al Udeid (Qatar)**, Muwaffaq al-Salti (**Jordan**), **Al Dhafra (UAE)** — fuel storage, hangars, barracks | ABC/Palestine Chronicle/CNN satellite reporting |
| Radar network hits across **five countries** | Gulf + Jordan | US broadcaster satellite analysis via Defence Security Asia |

### Claimed by Iran, not independently verified

- Patriot battery, Ali Al Salem AB, Kuwait (13 Jul) — battery had reportedly intercepted 97 projectiles, most of any GCC battery
- **AN/FPS-117, Ahmad al-Jaber AB, Kuwait (13 Jul and again 21 Jul — see §4)**
- AN/TPS-59, AN/MPQ-64, additional early-warning/missile-defense radars and SATCOM at Kuwait/Bahrain/Jordan bases (Nasr-2 / Sa'eqeh statements)
- Iranian-side aggregate claims: 228 assets damaged across 15–16 US bases; $2.3–2.8B equipment losses (Indian Defence News / Defence Security Asia — advocacy-adjacent outlets, treat with caution)

### Counter-attrition (context the "dwindling" narrative omits)

- US strikes have destroyed Iranian coastal/fixed radar sites at scale; a US official told WSJ Iran resorted to **small portable radars to replace destroyed fixed sites** (ISW/Critical Threats).
- Reuters (via Times of Israel): US can confirm only ~⅓ of Iran's missile arsenal destroyed — an example of *both* sides' BDA claims outrunning verification.
- Iran has reconstituted: 50%+ of pre-war missile/launcher stockpile accessible again (ISW).
